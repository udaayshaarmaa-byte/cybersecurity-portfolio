# Risk, Threats, Vulnerabilities & Asset Management

---

## 1. Core Security Terms | Precise Definitions

These three terms are often used interchangeably in everyday life | in security, they have specific meanings:

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| **Risk** | Anything that can impact the confidentiality, integrity, or availability of an asset |
| **Threat** | Any circumstance or event that can negatively impact assets |
| **Vulnerability** | A weakness that can be exploited by a threat |

### How they relate:
```
Threat exploits a Vulnerability → creates Risk to an Asset
```

**Risk formula:**
```
Likelihood × Impact = Risk
```

> {A nail on the road (threat) + thin tyres (vulnerability) = risk of a flat and being late to work (impact). Security planning = reducing either the likelihood or the impact side of that equation.}

---

## 2. Why We Calculate Risk

Risk calculation helps organisations:
- Prevent costly and disruptive events
- Identify improvements to systems and processes
- Determine which risks can be tolerated (risk appetite)
- Prioritise critical assets that require attention

**Primary focus as a security professional:** reduce the **likelihood** side of the equation by addressing threats and vulnerabilities.

---

## 3. Categories of Threats

Threats = circumstances or events that can negatively impact assets.

| Category | Description | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Intentional** | Deliberate malicious action | Attacker exploiting a misconfigured application to access sensitive data |
| **Unintentional** | Accidental or negligent action | Employee holds door open for an unknown person → unauthorised physical access |

---

## 4. Categories of Vulnerabilities

Vulnerabilities = weaknesses that can be exploited by threats.

| Category | Description | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Technical** | Weakness in systems, software, or configuration | Misconfigured software granting unauthorised access |
| **Human** | Weakness in people, processes, or behaviour | Employee loses their access card in a car park |

---

## 5. Asset Management

**Asset management** = the process of tracking assets and the risks that affect them.

**Core principle:** you can only protect what you know you have.

### Types of assets organisations protect:

| Asset Type | Examples |
|---|---|
| **Digital assets** | Customer data, financial records |
| **Information systems** | Networks, software, databases |
| **Physical assets** | Facilities, equipment, hardware, supplies |
| **Intangible assets** | Brand reputation, intellectual property |

---

## 6. Asset Classification

**Asset classification** = labelling assets based on sensitivity and importance.

To determine sensitivity and importance, you need to know:
- **What** you have
- **Where** it is
- **Who owns** it
- **How important** it is

### Classification Scheme (most common):

| Level | Description | Access |
|---|---|---|
| **Restricted** | Highest sensitivity  need-to-know only | Extremely limited |
| **Confidential** | Disclosure could cause significant negative impact | Limited to authorised individuals |
| **Internal-only** | Available to employees and business partners | Internal access |
| **Public** | No negative consequences if released | Anyone |

---

## 7. Why Asset Classification Matters

- Enables effective **risk management strategy**
- Helps **prioritise security resources** to what matters most
- Reduces **IT costs** by not over-protecting low-value assets
- Supports **legal and regulatory compliance**

---

## 8. Challenges of Classifying Assets

### Ownership complexity:
- A company laptop used by an employee for personal storage who owns the personal data on it?
- Identifying asset owners is often non-trivial

### Multiple classification values:
- A single document can contain both public information (your name) and confidential information (your address) simultaneously
- Context determines classification, not just content
